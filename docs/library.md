# Leverage Library

The curated front door to the reading behind this field guide. The full catalog lives in
[READING.md](reading.md), right alongside this page. This page is the shortlist: the books
worth your hour, organized by the idea they sharpen.

The thesis of everything here is the same as the repo's: **more leverage, not more AI.** These
books are leverage multipliers for your judgment, your focus, and your clarity. None of them
tell you to use more AI. They tell you to use *yourself* better, and to keep the machine in
its lane.

> **How this page works (same rules as the repo).** These books sit in a private library.
> This page cites and recommends. It does not host files, and it never links pirate sources.
> Ideas are attributed to authors; copyrighted text is not reproduced. The one exception is
> *Scientific Advertising* (Claude Hopkins, 1923), which is public domain in the US and may
> be quoted briefly with attribution. Everything else: buy it, borrow it, or use the free
> legit source noted per theme.

## Start here (ranked)

If you read nothing else from the library, read these ten, in this order. Each one earns its
place by feeding the "more leverage, not more AI" thesis directly.

1. **Peak** (Ericsson & Pool). Skills are *built*, not found; deliberate practice beats the
   10,000-hour myth, so your leverage is a habit you can engineer rather than a gift you wait for.
2. **Make It Stick** (Brown, Roediger, McDaniel). Retrieval and spacing are the most proven ways
   to actually retain what you learn, so your reading time compounds instead of leaking out.
3. **AI Snake Oil** (Narayanan & Kapoor). The most rigorous non-hype AI book in the library; it
   draws the bright line between real leverage and expensive theater.
4. **Co-Intelligence** (Mollick). Frames the model as a co-intelligence you steer; "be the human
   in the loop" is this repo's whole stance in four words.
5. **Deep Work** (Newport). Focused, distraction-free work is the scarce resource; protect it and
   the machine amplifies it instead of fragmenting it.
6. **The Scout Mindset** (Galef). Soldier vs. scout is a clean model for checking your own beliefs
   before you trust any output, human or machine.
7. **Crucial Conversations** (Grenny et al.). Its "safety" principle is exactly what a good
   maker-checker prompt reproduces: candor without pushing the other side away.
8. **The Mom Test** (Fitzpatrick). Teaches you to validate with real behavior, not polite
   opinions, so your AI prep questions surface signal instead of noise.
9. **Scientific Advertising** (Hopkins, 1923). The one public-domain book here; its test-and-
   measure doctrine is the root of every copy loop you will build (see the excerpt below).
10. **Good Strategy / Bad Strategy** (Rumelt). The diagnosis-then-action kernel is the backbone of
    "stop when the condition is met," not "stop when it feels done."

### Newest additions: the AI/LLM shelf

Pulled in as the highest-priority reading for the prompt and LLM guides. These four are the most
field-guide-ready books in the whole library.

- **Prompt Engineering for LLMs** (Berryman & Ziegler). The single most useful book here for
  writing prompts: models are next-token predictors, and few-shot examples plus retrieved context
  beat clever phrasing every time.
- **Hands-On Large Language Models** (Alammar & Grootendorst). The clearest non-engineer path
  from tokens to embeddings to retrieval-augmented generation, which is the durable, low-risk pattern.
- **AI: A Guide for Thinking Humans** (Mitchell). The accessible, rigorous caution: impressive
  output is not evidence of understanding, so verify before you trust.
- **The Hundred-Page Machine Learning Book** (Burkov). Author-distributed on a "read first, buy
  later" basis; the mental model that models are narrow formulas, not minds.

## Themes (grouped picks)

Each theme names the books that support it, with a one-line "why it matters," plus the legitimately
free sources that reinforce the idea. Themes map to the existing guides where the fit is direct.

### Theme A: Judgment over automation (human-in-the-loop)

A prediction is not a decision. The lever is keeping your judgment in the loop and verifying
before you believe.

- **AI Snake Oil** (Narayanan & Kapoor). Separates real generative progress from predictive AI
  that judges people and cannot work as advertised.
- **Co-Intelligence** (Mollick). The "Jagged Frontier" and four rules make human-in-the-loop a
  habit, not a slogan.
- **Prediction Machines** (Agrawal, Gans, Goldfarb). As prediction gets cheap, human judgment
  gets *more* valuable, not less.
- **Thinking in Bets** (Duke). Judge the process, not the outcome; decide what would change your
  mind before you start.

**Free, legit sources:** [AI Snake Oil companion (aisnakeoil.com)](https://www.aisnakeoil.com/),
[Elements of AI](https://www.elementsofai.com/),
[Google AI Essentials](https://www.coursera.org/learn/google-ai-essentials),
[Generative AI for Everyone (Andrew Ng)](https://www.deeplearning.ai/courses/generative-ai-for-everyone/).
**Pairs with:** [Stop Conditions](guides/stop-conditions.md).

### Theme B: Deliberate, retrieval-based learning (build leverage via skill)

Expertise is built through feedback-rich practice and retrieval, not through rereading or hours
logged. This is the spine of the learning-paths approach.

- **Make It Stick** (Brown, Roediger, McDaniel). Retrieval, spacing, and interleaving are the most
  replicated, defensible findings in learning science.
- **Peak** (Ericsson & Pool). Expertise is deliberate practice with feedback, not innate talent;
  the 10,000-hour rule is a myth.
- **How Learning Happens** (Kirschner & Hendrick). Instruction should follow replicated research,
  not intuition; retrieval is the most uncontested lever.
- **Practice Perfect** (Lemov et al.). "Practice makes permanent, not perfect," so encode success
  and shorten the feedback loop.

**Free, legit sources:** [The Learning Scientists (learningscientists.org)](https://www.learningscientists.org/),
[Learning How to Learn (Coursera, free audit)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/learning-how-to-learn).
**Pairs with:** [Learning Paths](guides/learning-paths.md).

### Theme C: Less-for-more leverage systems (operator systems)

Eliminating and automating low-leverage work returns more than any new tool. This is the
operating logic behind recurring prompts.

- **Deep Work** (Newport). Distraction-free, cognitively demanding work is what creates value and
  is hard to replicate.
- **Essentialism** (McKeown). The disciplined pursuit of the vital few; invest effort where it
  returns the most.
- **The 8020 Manager** (Koch). Roughly 20% of causes produce 80% of results; find and protect
  that 20%.
- **A World Without Email** (Newport). The "Hyperactive Hive Mind" of constant messaging is the
  real enemy of focused leverage.

**Free, legit sources:** free Getting Things Done overviews, e.g.
[Getting Things Done](https://www.gettingthingsdone.com/) (David Allen's method, the captain's
stated productivity anchor; linkable summaries only, not the book).
**Pairs with:** [Recurring Prompts](guides/recurring-prompts.md).

### Theme D: Clear, point-first writing (status & follow-ups)

Write the busy reader's job for them: point first, low effort, lead with the action.

- **Writing for Busy Readers** (Rogers & Lasky-Fink). Six eye-tracking-tested principles for
  readers who will not read much; the most evidence-backed of the set.
- **Smart Brevity** (VandeHei, Allen, Schwartz). "Brevity is confidence. Length is fear." One big
  thing, then why it matters.
- **On Writing Well** (Zinsser). Strip every word that does not do necessary work; clutter is the
  disease of weak writing.
- **Get to the Point** (Schwartzberg). Share a stance, not just a topic; use PREP and
  problem-solution-benefit.

**Free, legit sources:** [Plain Language guidelines (plainlanguage.gov)](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/).
**Pairs with:** [Loop Prompting](guides/loop-prompting.md) and
[Recurring Prompts](guides/recurring-prompts.md).

### Theme E: High-stakes conversations & maker-checker splits

A good "checker" is a feedback loop instantiated as a prompt: candid, evidence-based, and safe.

- **Crucial Conversations** (Grenny et al.). Safety (mutual purpose plus respect) is the pivot
  variable in any hard conversation.
- **Radical Candor** (Scott). Care personally *and* challenge directly; withheld feedback degrades
  teams.
- **The Mom Test** (Fitzpatrick). Ask about the customer's life and past behavior, not their
  opinions or hypotheticals.
- **Thanks for the Feedback** (Stone & Heen). Separate appreciation, coaching, and evaluation so
  the feedback actually lands.

**Free, legit sources:** [The Mom Test cheatsheet (momtestbook.com)](https://momtestbook.com/),
[Radical Candor (radicalcandor.com)](https://www.radicalcandor.com/),
[Crucial Learning (crucialskills.com)](https://www.crucialskills.com/).
**Pairs with:** [Maker-Checker](guides/maker-checker.md).

### Theme F: Strategy as diagnosis + stop conditions (research prep)

Strategy starts with a diagnosis of the obstacle, never with goals. "Stop when X" is that
diagnosis made operational.

- **Good Strategy / Bad Strategy** (Rumelt). The kernel: diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent
  action. Fluff is not strategy.
- **The Crux** (Rumelt). Start with the most important *addressable* challenge, not with goals.
- **Thinking in Systems** (Meadows). Change systems at leverage points (information flows, goals),
  not at parameters.
- **Blue Ocean Strategy** (Kim & Mauborgne). Compete by creating uncontested space through value
  innovation.

**Free, legit sources:** [Harvard Business Review strategy articles (hbr.org)](https://hbr.org/),
[Blue Ocean Strategy resources (blueoceanstrategy.com)](https://www.blueoceanstrategy.com/).
**Pairs with:** [Stop Conditions](guides/stop-conditions.md).

### Theme G: Memory & persuasion (messaging that sticks)

Engineer memorability and honest persuasion. Persuasion must serve the reader's interest, not
manipulate (this loops back to Theme A's judgment rule).

- **Made to Stick** (Heath & Heath). Sticky ideas are Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible,
  Emotional, and tell a Story.
- **Contagious** (Berger). Six STEPPS drive word of mouth; quality and price alone do not.
- **Influence** (Cialdini). Seven principles of persuasion, grounded in social psychology.
- **Scientific Advertising** (Hopkins, 1923, public domain). The root "test-and-measure /
  reason-why" doctrine of all copycraft.

> **Public-domain excerpt (Hopkins, 1923, US public domain):** *"Advertising is salesmanship in
> print."* Everything else in this book is the same doctrine applied: state the reason-why, then
> test the result and keep what measures. Because the book is public domain in the US, it is the
> one title here safe to quote and to read free from a legitimate source.

**Free, legit sources:** [Scientific Advertising (Internet Archive, public domain)](https://archive.org/details/scientificadvert0000hopk),
[Cialdini's principles (influenceatwork.com)](https://www.influenceatwork.com/),
[The Gary Halbert Letters (free)](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/).
**Pairs with:** [Recurring Prompts](guides/recurring-prompts.md) for copy-paste drafting prompts.

### Theme H: Discovery with real signals (avoid false validation)

Validate with behavior and commitment, not opinions. A guardrail for the whole repo: do not
ship a "free resource" without checking it is actually free and legit.

- **Continuous Discovery Habits** (Torres). Discovery is a continuous team habit; test assumptions,
  not ideas, and interview customers on a cadence.
- **The Mom Test** (Fitzpatrick). Bad customer conversations are worse than none; talk about their
  life, not your idea.
- **The Effortless Experience** (Dixon et al.). Reducing customer effort drives loyalty more than
  delight does.
- **Customer Success** (Mehta et al.). In subscriptions, your success is tied to the customer's
  ongoing success.

**Free, legit sources:** [Continuous Discovery Habits (ProductTalk.org)](https://www.producttalk.org/),
[The Mom Test cheatsheet (momtestbook.com)](https://momtestbook.com/).
**Pairs with:** [Stop Conditions](guides/stop-conditions.md) and the repo's "what's real vs. hype"
filter.

## Reading roadmap

Use the library in three passes, not all at once:

1. **Ranked picks first.** Read the ten "Start here" books (and the AI/LLM shelf) before anything
   else. They are the highest-leverage ideas and the ones the guides lean on hardest.
2. **Theme picks next.** When a specific job shows up, go to its theme. Prepping a status update?
   Theme D. Building a checker prompt? Theme E. Setting a stop condition? Theme F.
3. **Full catalog last.** [READING.md](reading.md)
   is the complete inventory when you want to go deep or find the primary source for a claim.

The guides are the practice; the library is the reading that makes the practice stick. Start with
a guide, hit a wall, then pull the book behind it.
