# Learning Paths

*Build skill that sticks by running retrieval and spacing as prompt loops. The capture, structure, judgment loop, applied to your own brain.*

## Old way

You read the book, highlight the good parts, maybe take notes. A week later it is gone. You "consumed" the content but you did not change. Rereading feels like learning, and it mostly isn't. Recognition (it looked familiar) gets mistaken for knowing.

## Swap

Treat learning like the loop-prompting guide treats writing: a reps loop, not a consumption event. The research here is blunt. Retrieving what you know (testing yourself) beats rereading. Spacing those retrievals out beats cramming. Interleaving topics beats blocking one. And the model is a near-perfect retrieval partner: it can quiz you, adapt, and never tire of your third attempt.

Make It Stick (Brown, Roediger, McDaniel) is the evidence base: retrieval, spacing, and interleaving are the most replicated findings in learning science. Peak (Ericsson & Pool) adds that expertise is built through deliberate practice, reps with feedback that build mental representations, not through talent or hours alone. How Learning Happens (Kirschner & Hendrick) calls retrieval "one of the most uncontested areas of research on learning." Dehaene, in How We Learn, frames the four pillars of the brain's learning algorithm: attention, active engagement, error feedback, and consolidation (sleep and spacing). Ultralearning (Scott Young) supplies the structure: metalearning (map the skill before you start), directness (practice the real task, not a proxy), and spaced retrieval for retention.

## The loop

```
1. METALARN:   before you study, map the skill. What does "good" look like, and what are the sub-skills?
2. RETRIEVE:   close the source. Ask the model to quiz you, or write down what you remember cold.
3. FEEDBACK:   compare your answer to the source. The error is the lesson, not a failure.
4. SPACE:      wait. Run the same retrieval again a day later, then three days, then a week.
5. INTERLEAVE: mix topics instead of blocking one. The model can shuffle the quiz order.
6. CONSOLIDATE: summarize in your own words, then sleep on it.
```

The key is step 2. Most people reread, which feels productive and barely moves the needle. Retrieval is uncomfortable precisely because it is working. The model's job is to force the retrieval and to grade it honestly.

## Copy-paste template

```
CONTEXT: I'm learning [skill or topic]. Here is what I've read so far (or paste your notes).
TASK: Quiz me. Ask 5 questions that force me to retrieve from memory, not recognize.
  - 3 recall questions (define it / explain it / show the steps)
  - 2 applied questions (here is a scenario, what do you do?)
After I answer, grade me, point to the exact gap, and give one retrieval prompt for tomorrow.
CONSTRAINTS: Don't tell me the answer up front. Make me produce it.
```

Run it. Answer cold. Then run again tomorrow with "quiz me again, harder." That spaced retrieval is where the skill actually lands. Save the template as a recurring prompt so the spacing happens on schedule, not when you remember to.

## What changes

- Reading becomes reps, so it sticks instead of evaporating.
- You stop confusing recognition with knowing.
- The model becomes a tutor that never tires of your third attempt.
- Learning a new tool or workflow gets a path instead of a hope.

## Tradeoff

It is slower than skimming and it feels less productive in the moment. The discipline is trusting the research over the feeling: retrieval is hard because it is the rep that builds the muscle. Match the loop to the stakes: a compliance cert gets spaced retrieval; a podcast you will forget by Friday does not. And keep judgment in: the model can quiz you, but you decide what is worth learning in the first place (see Maker-Checker).

### Exercise 1: Retrieval Log for One Book (Week 5)

Pick ONE book from the [Leverage Library](library.md) Start Here list. Run the retrieval loop:

```
1. METALARN: Before you study, map the skill. What does "good" look like? What are the sub-skills?
2. RETRIEVE: Close the source. Write what you remember cold. Or ask the model to quiz you.
3. FEEDBACK: Compare your answer to the source. The error is the lesson.
4. SPACE: Wait. Run the same retrieval again in 1 day, 3 days, 1 week.
5. INTERLEAVE: Mix topics. The model can shuffle the quiz order.
6. CONSOLIDATE: Summarize in your own words. Sleep on it.
```

**Log Format:**
```
BOOK: [Title] — [Author]
DATE STARTED: ___________

METALARN (pre-study):
- What "good" looks like for this book's ideas:
- Sub-skills I want to extract:

SESSION 1 (Day 0) — RETRIEVE + FEEDBACK:
- What I recalled cold:
- What the source says (key differences):
- Error lesson:

SESSION 2 (Day 1) — RETRIEVE:
- What I recalled:

SESSION 3 (Day 3) — RETRIEVE:
- What I recalled:

SESSION 4 (Day 7) — RETRIEVE + CONSOLIDATE:
- What I recalled:
- My one-paragraph summary in my own words:
- How I'll use this in my work:

TIME SPENT TOTAL: ______ minutes
```

**Stop condition:** All 4 sessions logged. One-paragraph summary written. Applied once to work.
