The Leverage Loop

The core engine of AI leverage: Capture → Structure → Judgment → Re-run → Repeat. Your judgment is the product, not the AI output.

The One-Sentence Definition

AI is a leverage layer, not a replacement. You capture messy context, let the machine structure a draft, apply your judgment to fix what's wrong, feed that back, and repeat until your stop condition passes. The edit is the leverage — and saving that loop makes it compound.

The Five Steps

1

Capture

Dump everything: messy notes, bullet points, meeting transcripts, half-formed thoughts, raw data. Don't organize. Don't filter. Just get it out of your head and into the prompt. The messier the input, the more leverage you gain by offloading the structuring.

2

Structure

Run a prompt that organizes the mess into a usable draft — a status update, a follow-up email, a meeting prep doc, a brief. The prompt does the heavy lifting: formatting, grouping, drafting. You get a first pass in seconds instead of minutes.

3

Judgment Key Step

This is where the leverage lives. You read the draft and mark what's wrong, missing, off-voice, or hallucinated. Every factual claim needs a source. Every number must trace to a document. The ask must be explicit in the first two lines. Unverified claims get flagged. This is not "vibes" — it's a checklist. If you skip this, you're not leveraging AI; you're outsourcing your reputation.

4

Re-run

Feed your judgment back as the next prompt: "Fix these three things: [your list]. Keep everything else." The model refines. You review again. Typical tasks take 2–4 loops. Each loop is cheaper than writing from scratch, and the quality converges toward your standard.

5

Repeat

When your stop condition passes (see Stop Conditions), ship it. Then save the prompt + judgment pattern as a recurring prompt. Next week, you start at Step 2 with a template that already encodes your preferences. That's how leverage compounds.

Why Human Review Matters

A prediction is not a decision.

AI predicts probable text. You decide what's true, what's useful, and what goes out with your name on it. The machine drafts; you judge. That division of labor is the entire point. If you forward AI output without checking it, you're not using AI — you're being used by it.

  • Hallucinations are features, not bugs — the model predicts plausible-sounding text. Only you know what's actually true.
  • Voice is not style — "professional tone" is a proxy. Your actual voice is the specific phrases, caveats, and framing you'd use.
  • Risk is asymmetric — a bad status update wastes 5 minutes of reading. A bad strategic brief wastes weeks of execution. Your judgment scales with the stakes.
  • The edit is the signal — every correction you make teaches the next loop. Saved corrections become your prompt library.

Realistic Professional Example

Scenario: Weekly Status Update for a Manager

Capture (2 min): Paste Slack threads, Jira ticket links, meeting notes, one-pager from last week.

Structure (15 sec): Run your saved "status update" prompt. Get a draft with: status line + ask, 3 bullets (moved/blocked/next), under 200 words.

Judgment (3 min): Check: Is the ask explicit? Does every number trace to a ticket? Are blocked items actually blocked? Is tone right for your skip-level? Flag 2 unverified claims.

Re-run (10 sec): "Fix the two flagged claims — add ticket links. Make the ask bolder." Get refined draft.

Repeat: Stop condition passes (ask explicit, every number traced). Send. Save any new corrections to your prompt library.

Total time: ~6 minutes vs. 20+ minutes writing from scratch. Next week: 4 minutes.

Stop-Condition Checklist

Before you ship, every item must be true:

  • The ask is explicit and in the first two lines
  • Every factual claim has a source
  • Every number traces to a source document
  • Unverified claims are explicitly flagged
  • All requested sections are present
  • Nothing missing that was in the original request

Common Mistakes

Accepting the First Draft

The first output is a starting point, not a finish line. Plan for 2–4 loops.

Vague Stop Conditions

"Until it looks good" passes everything. Define observable, falsifiable criteria up front.

Skipping the Judgment Log

If you don't record what you fixed, you can't save it as a recurring prompt. Leverage doesn't compound.

Prompt Theater

Clever phrasing beats structure? No. Few-shot + grounded context beats clever phrasing every time.

Try It Now — Exercise

Exercise 1.2: Run the Loop 3× This Week

Pick one recurring task you do weekly. Run the Leverage Loop on it three times this week. Save the prompt and your judgment notes. That's your first recurring prompt.

  1. Choose: status update, follow-up email, meeting prep, or brief
  2. Capture: paste your raw notes into the prompt template
  3. Structure: run the prompt, get a draft
  4. Judgment: mark every issue with a checklist (not vibes)
  5. Re-run: feed fixes back, repeat until stop condition passes
  6. Save: copy the final prompt + judgment pattern to your library

Deliverable: A saved recurring prompt + judgment log for one real task.
