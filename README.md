# AI Leverage Field Guide

**Practical AI leverage for working professionals — managers, operators, knowledge workers.
Not engineers. Not hype. More leverage, not more AI.**

This repo is two things:

1. **A curated hub** (this README) of the best *free, legitimate* AI resources for
   professionals — courses, tools with real free tiers, official docs, free reading, and
   prompt/workflow libraries. Vendor-neutral. No affiliate links. No paywalls.
2. **A guides site** (`/docs`) of short, copy-paste original guides written from inside
   real work — the exact material most "AI resources" leave out.

Everything here is free to read and free to share. The point isn't to use *more* AI. It's to
get more output per hour without adding hours — and to keep your judgment in the loop where it
matters. No tool treadmill. No prompt theater. No outsourced judgment.

> This repo is **private until launch**. When it goes public, the links below should all be
> free and live. A link-checker CI will run on every PR. If you find a dead or paywalled
> link, open an issue.

## Start here (your first win in 5 minutes)

You don't need a course. Do these three things:

1. **Pick one recurring piece of work** you do weekly — a status update, a follow-up email,
   a meeting-prep note.
2. **Open a free chat tool** (see [Tools](#free-tools-with-real-free-tiers)) and paste this:
   ```
   I'm a [your role]. Here are my messy notes for this week's [status update / follow-up].
   Turn them into a tight version: first line = the status + the ask, then 3 bullets
   (what moved / what's blocked / what's next). Plain language. Under 200 words.
   ```
3. **Read the result like a boss, not a rubber stamp.** Fix anything wrong, off-voice, or
   missing. That edit *is* the leverage. Save the prompt (see
   [Recurring Prompts](docs/guides/recurring-prompts.md)).

That's the whole loop. The guides below make it sharper.

## Free AI courses

Free, no certificate-paywall required (audit tracks are fine). Picked for non-engineers who
want literacy, not a degree.

- [Elements of AI](https://www.elementsofai.com/) — Univ. of Helsinki & MinnaLearn. The
  friendliest "what AI actually is" course for non-technical people. No math.
- [Google AI Essentials](https://www.coursera.org/learn/google-ai-essentials) — Coursera
  audit track. Practical everyday-use basics from Google.
- [Generative AI for Everyone](https://www.deeplearning.ai/courses/generative-ai-for-everyone/)
  — Andrew Ng / DeepLearning.AI. Plain-English mental models, zero code.
- [AI For Everyone](https://www.deeplearning.ai/courses/ai-for-everyone/) — Andrew Ng.
  How AI fits organizations; great for managers deciding where it's real.
- [Harvard CS50's Intro to AI with Python](https://cs50.harvard.edu/ai/) — Free
  OpenCourseWare. Rigorous; best if you'll touch a little code.
- [Microsoft Generative AI for Beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/generative-ai-for-beginners)
  — 18-lesson, MIT-licensed GitHub course. Free, no account.
- [Microsoft AI for Beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/AI-For-Beginners) — 24-lesson
  curriculum on how ML works. Free, open source.
- [Cohere LLM University](https://cohere.com/llmu) — Free modules on how LLMs work and how
  to build with them. Slightly technical; good if you want the internals.
- [Khan Academy — AI for Education / Intro to AI](https://www.khanacademy.org/ai-for-education)
  — Free, approachable intro to what AI is and how it works.
- [Google Cloud — Introduction to Generative AI](https://www.cloudskillsboost.google/course_templates/536)
  — Free short course on Cloud Skills Boost.
- [Hugging Face NLP Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/nlp-course) — Free, code-leaning
  intro to modern NLP. For the curious operator, not the coder.
- [IBM AI Foundations (audit)](https://www.coursera.org/professional-certificates/ibm-ai-foundations) —
  Coursera audit track. Broad AI/ML literacy from IBM.

## Free tools with real free tiers

These have a genuinely usable free tier — not a trial that locks you out in a week.

- [ChatGPT](https://chat.openai.com) — OpenAI's chat. Free tier handles most daily leverage.
- [Claude](https://claude.ai) — Anthropic's chat. Strong at long documents and careful writing.
- [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com) — Google's chat, with deep Docs/Workspace integration.
- [Microsoft Copilot](https://copilot.microsoft.com) — Free chat with web grounding.
- [Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai) — Answer engine with cited sources; good for research.
- [NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com) — Ground a chatbot in *your* documents. Excellent
  for meeting prep and summarizing your own files.
- [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co) — Free models, datasets, and Spaces to try AI directly.
- [Poe](https://poe.com) — One front door to several models, free tier included.
- [Mistral Le Chat](https://chat.mistral.ai) — Free European-model chat.
- [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md) — Free for personal use. Where your prompt library lives
  (pair with the [Recurring Prompts](docs/guides/recurring-prompts.md) guide).
- [Whisper](https://github.com/openai/whisper) — Open-source speech-to-text. Free, local,
  turn meetings/voice notes into text to feed the loop.

## Hype vs. real — a quick filter

Most "AI" content is either a course funnel or a demo. Use this filter before you spend time:

- **Real** = a specific, repeatable job you can do this week (summarize this, draft that,
  check this). Free tier, no account needed to learn it.
- **Hype** = "revolutionize your workflow," "10x everything," a tool that needs a 40-min
  video to explain, or anything asking for your card before you've seen the value.
- **Outsourced judgment** = any setup where the machine makes the call and you just forward
  it. That's not leverage; that's liability. Keep the human in the loop (see
  [Maker-Checker](docs/guides/maker-checker.md)).

If a resource can't pass "what exactly will I do with this on Monday?" — skip it.
